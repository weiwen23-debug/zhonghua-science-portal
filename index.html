💧 Float or Sink?

Investigate what happens when spoons made of different materials are placed in water.

Science challenge: Compare spoons made from metal, plastic, glass, rubber, wood, fabric and ceramic. The fabric sample is a spoon-shaped cotton cutout, not a rigid usable spoon. Results are illustrative for these particular samples: plastic and rubber vary by composition, and fabric may float briefly before becoming wet. Shape, trapped air and water absorption also affect floating. Test real samples with your teacher to check these predictions.

1. Choose an object

Choose a spoon made from each of the seven materials. All spoon illustrations have the same size and shape. Tap a spoon card below to select it, then make a prediction. Dragging is optional.

Why should the spoons be the same size and shape when we investigate how the material affects floating or sinking?

💪 Investigation 2: Strength of Materials

How many identical weights will cause each material strip to break or tear?

About this simulation: Illustrative simulation, not measured data. Each strip has the same modelled length, width and thickness, and each weight has the same mass. The materials are specific examples, including thin aluminium foil for metal. Real results depend on the sample, grip, thickness, defects and testing method. Glass and ceramic are brittle and need specialist handling in real tests. Do not treat this ranking as a universal fact about material strength.

1. Choose a material

Choose one sample strip. Every virtual strip has the same dimensions. Only the material changes.

🔎 Fair-test question

Why should the strips have the same length, width and thickness?

2. Predict

Predict which added weight will cause the strip to break or tear (1–12).

3. Test the strip

Add identical weights one at a time. Count the weight that causes the strip to break or tear.
